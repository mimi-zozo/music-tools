Frank Dawson · Howard Roberts · Maxwell MaltzThe 18-minute practice process

One element. Eighteen minutes. A five-minute break. Every day for twenty-one days. The rest of the method exists to protect that loop.

Re-written by Dave Benziger from an article by his teacher Frank Dawson at the American Conservatory of Music, Chicago. The original context was the jazz guitar combo course, where students had to know the 7 positions of the Major scale and the 7 positions of the Melodic Minor scale, the "14 scales". Dave's note: it works for anything, including pentatonics, lead parts and fingerstyle patterns.

Why it is built this way

The brain is a goal-seeking device. Keep the long-range goals (being a monster player) on the back burner and point it only at an immediate, small target, because the input circuits take small amounts at a time. Any success, however tiny, produces a positive response, and a positive response makes the brain seek the next positive goal. So the element is kept small on purpose: not because you're slow, but to guarantee yourself success.

Everything in music is built from scales. The target standard is being able to play a scale and hold a conversation with a friend at the same time.

One practice session

  1. Study it away from the guitar

    ~15 MIN · NO GUITAR

    Take one scale pattern, just one. Get the fingerings, string numbers, extensions, everything into your head before you pick up the instrument. Take as long as you need; fifteen minutes is normally enough.

  2. Set a timer for 18 minutes

    18:00 · TIMER REQUIRED

    Howard Roberts' rule from his "A" seminar: never less than 15, never more than 20. Frank picked 18 because it sits in the middle. Use a clock, a stove timer, anything that will cue you.

  3. Play it slow, clear, legato, loud

    SlowClearLegatoLoud

    A "simple" scale involves hundreds of tactical decisions: which finger, which string, angle of attack, pressure. You are programming an acquired reflex, and if you play it wrong you program it wrong. It takes 20 to 100 repetitions to lay down the first layer of motor response, and that first layer is all this block is for. By the end of 18 minutes you should be "roaring" through it.

    Don't over-repeat initially. Too many layers at once distort the image.

  4. When it dings, stop. Take at least 5 minutes off.

    ≥ 5:00 · GUITAR DOWN, WALK AWAY

    The urge will be "I just got into it!" Don't. Put the guitar down gently, get up and get away from it.

    Frank's picture: new information enters a nerve cell like an exposure on a photographic plate, delivered across the synapse. The break lets the image develop instead of being smeared by more layers of motor input.

  5. After the break, play as long as you like

    1–5 MIN PER PATTERN · DAILY FOR 21 DAYS

    Any amount of time is fine here, but the element must be touched once a day for 21 days. Maltz calls this a "bio-cycle" in Psycho-Cybernetics. From day two onward, every earlier pattern gets the same post-break time as the new one, so nothing learned is left to fade.

The 21-day schedule

Each day adds one new pattern to the 18-minute block. After the break, run every pattern you have learned so far for 1 to 5 minutes each, newest first. By day 7 the post-break block covers all seven positions; keep cycling through day 21.

Eight ways to fill the 18 minutes

Play the day’s pattern…

  1. IAt every possible fret position Straight (as 8th notes / 2 notes per beat)
  2. IIAt every possible fret position as Triplets (3 notes per beat)
  3. IIIRepeat I using Hammer-Ons ascending and Pull-Offs descending
  4. IVRepeat II using Hammer-Ons ascending and Pull-Offs descending
  5. VRepeat I using Right-Hand Muting
  6. VIRepeat II using Right-Hand Muting
  7. VIIRepeat III using Right-Hand Muting
  8. VIIIRepeat IV using Right-Hand Muting

18 minutes / 8 approaches = 2 minutes 15 seconds on each approach and you’re done! Or add some of your own ideas.

Rules of the method

Do
  • One pattern per day, fully memorised before you touch the guitar.
  • Use a real timer. 18 minutes, then stop.
  • Slow, clear, legato, loud.
  • Walk away for at least 5 minutes.
  • Touch every learned pattern every day for 21 days.
Don't
  • Try to learn all 14 scales in one session. You will never succeed.
  • Punch out the timer and keep going because you "just got into it".
  • Play it sloppy to go faster. You are programming the error.
  • Over-repeat in the first block. 20–100 reps is the first layer; more smears it.
  • Skip a day inside the 21.
